# Squeeze the hopper, leave the drivetrain alone

Automatic drivetrain shaking is not worth adding to Team 5669's robot as a standard agitation layer. No 2026 source reports a measured throughput gain from it. The teams that discuss it call it a driver workaround for a bad hopper throat. On this robot it would also defeat the team's own shooter logic: any chassis rotation faster than about 0.52 rad/s forces the turret past the 30 deg/s READY limit, and even a small translation shuffle swings the shoot-while-moving lead point by several degrees, so ShootCommand would pause feeding during the very motion meant to help it. The stronger pattern comes from Team 581's published code, which squeezes the hopper with the intake for the whole scoring state and keeps the intake roller turning forward at 5 V while firing. That keeps arches from forming, where a pump only reacts after a gap has already cost balls. Lab silo research agrees: vibration breaks arches after they form but does not stop them forming, and the strongest arches need a bigger kick. Two changes buy the most: shorten the 0.8 s gap trigger to about 0.4 s and count it only while ShootCommand is actually feeding, and add a preventive squeeze plus roller-forward layer under the reactive ladder you already have. The motors and gearboxes have wide torque margins at the current limits. The real risks are impact at the ends of pivot travel, heat from repeated stalls, and battery sag. A soft pump profile, a rolling rate cap, a temperature guard and a battery-voltage gate cover those. Drivetrain shuffling should stay behind a default-off flag: stationary only, translation only, lead compensation off.

## Drivetrain shaking is a forum workaround with no measured results

The direct evidence is thin, and all of it is anecdotal. The one REBUILT thread on the topic, "Shake the Robot" (March 13, 2026), comes from a swerve team (MK4i L2) whose driver rotates the robot back and forth with the right stick to clear **two balls fighting at the shooter tunnel entrance** ([Chief Delphi](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/shake-the-robot/516274)). The replies offer untested sketches: a robot-centric translation shuffle at `0.5 * cos(30 t)` m/s (about **4.8 Hz, about 1.7 cm peak displacement**), a constant 3 rad/s spin on a button, and a repeating clockwise/counterclockwise sequence. Most posters say to fix the geometry first. One recommends detecting a jam from feeder current against speed and **reversing the belts for about 0.5 s** instead of shaking ([Chief Delphi](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/shake-the-robot/516274)). Nobody posted balls per second before and after. The one first-hand outcome is negative: a Reddit poster whose driver shakes the robot side to side to feed reports that it **hurts shot accuracy** ([Reddit snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1rlw9xj/snapshots/2026-03-06T15%3A24%3A36.53383Z)). In a throughput thread, a team whose powered cam floor stalled with a full hopper says "drivers still shook the robot," and another poster suggests vibrating the drivetrain in place while admitting he is "uncertain how well it works" ([Chief Delphi](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/ways-to-push-throughput/516939)).

Better-resourced teams agitate with mechanisms instead. FRC 157 planned an intake-pivot "wiggle/agitate" button ([Chief Delphi](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/frc-157-the-aztechs-2026-build-thread-open-alliance/509425?page=4)). FRC 1719 runs a "Blender Motor" under its fuel storage ([GitHub](https://github.com/FRCteam1719theUmbrellaCorporation/1719-Rebuilt-2026)). 7769 found that "jostling the intake in and out would help keep fuel from becoming jammed in the hopper" ([Chief Delphi](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/521008)). The research had one coverage gap: GitHub code search was unavailable, so a 2026 repo with a drive-agitate command could exist without being found. Even so, no top-team build thread or code release surfaced one.

## Every drivetrain wiggle collides with 5669's READY gate

The case against drive shaking on this robot is specific to its software. The facts below come from the team repo (`/home/claude/2026-5669-official/CLAUDE.md`). ShootCommand feeds only while the turret is **within 2 deg and slower than 30 deg/s**. AimCommand uses feedforward to cancel robot spin. VisionLocalizer skips fixes while the robot spins faster than **2.5 rad/s**. The turret has about **300 deg of travel** and a dead zone behind its wires. Aim leads the hub by robot velocity times time of flight (`kShootWhileMoving`).

The engineering consequences follow directly from those numbers. They are inferences, not sourced results. A **rotation wiggle** makes the turret counter-rotate at the chassis rate. Any oscillation faster than **0.52 rad/s (30 deg/s)** breaks the READY gate, so feeding stops during the shake and resumes after it, and the shake adds nothing. The 3 rad/s spin from the forum also blinds vision and sweeps the hub through the dead zone. A **translation shuffle** is gentler in raw geometry: ±1.2 cm of lateral motion at 3 m is about 0.2 deg of aim change and about 6 deg/s of turret rate. The lead term breaks it anyway. At ±0.3 m/s and a 0.7 s flight time, the lead point swings about ±0.21 m, roughly **±4 deg at 3 m, oscillating at 4 Hz**, which works out to about 100 deg/s of commanded turret motion. That fails both READY conditions. A shuffle therefore helps only if lead compensation is off, or fed a low-passed velocity, while the shuffle runs. Wheel scrub during the reversals also corrupts odometry, which the aim depends on.

The electrical cost is a second problem. WPILib says a fresh battery has trouble sustaining **about 240 A for more than a second or two** and budgets around 180 A to stay above 7 V. The roboRIO 2 disables CAN motor controllers at a default **6.75 V** and keeps them off until the voltage recovers above 7.5 V ([WPILib](https://docs.wpilib.org/es/stable/docs/software/roborio-info/roborio-brownouts.html)). CTRE measured about **280 A when four swerve drive motors accelerate together**, and wheel slip near 130 A stator ([CTRE](https://phoenix-6-testing.readthedocs.io/en/latest/docs/hardware-reference/talonfx/improving-performance-with-current-limits.html)). Each wiggle reversal is an acceleration event. A repo finding matters here: CTRE swerve overwrites the drive stator limit with `SlipCurrent` ([CTRE source](https://api.ctr-electronics.com/phoenix6/release/cpp/_swerve_module_8hpp_source.html)), so 5669's drive motors run at **120 A stator, not the 80 A written in TunerConstants**. A wiggle stacked on flywheel recovery, a roller pulse and a pivot pump approaches the brownout case. A brownout drops the flywheel and the feeder too, so it costs more shots than it frees.

## Squeeze first, pulse second: prevent arches instead of waiting for gaps

The physics favors prevention. In a vibrated silo, vibration left the probability of arch formation unchanged (**p = 0.981 with and without**) but raised the probability of breaking an arch from **0 to 0.836**. "Some arches survive the vibration" and need "a stronger external perturbation" ([arXiv 0907.4871](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/0907.4871)). A second study found that jam durations under weak vibration follow a **power law whose mean may not converge**, so waiting is an unreliable strategy ([arXiv 0907.0114](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/0907.0114)). These are millimeter grains vibrated at 110-350 Hz, so the numbers do not transfer to 15 cm foam FUEL. The structural lessons do transfer: arches form when the outlet is only a few grains wide, gentle agitation clears most of them, and the robust ones need escalation. On Chief Delphi, spindexer bridging is described as "far more a ball's friction against each other problem" than a slope problem. A star-wheel tower where the balls bridge "helped tremendously," and a smaller inlet with a top ball blocker "eliminated inlet jams" ([Chief Delphi](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/spindexer-problem/517335)).

Team 581's comp-bot code is the clearest software model ([team581/frc-2026](https://github.com/team581/frc-2026)). In SCORE, the intake goes to a pre-squeeze position and, after a tunable **CompactionDelay of 0.7 s**, drives to a near-retracted compaction position and **holds it for the whole score state**. The intake roller keeps running **forward at 5 V while shooting**. 581's auto-unjam branch handles a jam differently from 5669: on a 0.25 s debounced jam sensor, it **ejects with the intake roller at -12 V while the conveyor and feeder keep feeding forward**, so the clearing motion pulls balls away from the jam instead of pushing them back into it. One caution from Chief Delphi: repeatedly retracting the intake helps, "but not during shoot-on-the-move" ([Chief Delphi](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/spindexer-problem/517335)), because a raised intake cannot collect.

Timing is where 5669's current ladder loses the most balls. This is an inference from your parameters. At 8 BPS a ball leaves every 0.125 s, so the **0.8 s trigger gives up about 6 balls** before agitation starts. A first pump of two cycles with 0.45 s legs then adds up to about 1.6 s more. A trigger near **0.4 s (about 3 ball intervals)** cuts that by half. Because 5669's first reactive step is cheap, false triggers cost little. One pitfall is a trap for the current design: if the gap timer runs while ShootCommand is paused waiting for aim, an aim pause looks like a starved hopper and fires a pump. The timer should count only while READY is true and the feeders are commanded forward. The existing per-ball RPM-dip meter ("Shots At Speed") already provides the "a ball left" signal. Spindexer speed sets the ceiling. At 16:1 the 8 in rim runs about 4 m/s, and a rolled ball moves at roughly half its contact surface speed ([Chief Delphi](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/spindexer-gearing/516580)). By inference that gives a ceiling of about 13 BPS for touching balls, comfortably above the "about 10 Fuel/s is table stakes" bar ([Chief Delphi](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/stop-overcomplicating-rebuilt/513157)). The limit is flow, not wheel speed.

## Current limits already protect the gearboxes; impacts and heat are the real risks

The gearboxes have large torque margins at the current limits. The Thrifty Cycloidal is **23:1** and is rated for **about 75 Nm usable output and about 350 Nm locked-input static load**. It survived **15,000+ full-reverse cycles at 25 Nm output** ([Thrifty Bot](https://thethriftybot.com/products/thrifty-cycloidal-gearbox)). A non-FOC Kraken X60 makes **7.09 Nm at 366 A** ([WCP](https://docs.wcproducts.com/welcome/electronics/kraken-x60/kraken-x60-motor/overview-and-features/motor-performance)), about 0.0194 Nm per amp. By inference, the pivot's **40 A stator limit gives about 14 Nm per gearbox**, roughly a fifth of the usable rating. At about 70 A it reaches the 25 Nm reversal-test load, so keeping the pivot at or under 60-70 A keeps every pump inside tested conditions. A full stall (about 130 Nm) is still under the static rating. Current cannot break the cycloidal. **Impact can**: an arm that hits the floor or a hard stop at speed is decelerated by rotor and arm inertia, reflected through 23:1, which a current limit does not cap. The drop leg of every pump must be a profiled move to DOWN plus the backoff, never a cut to neutral. On the spindexer, REV rates the **4:1 MAXPlanetary cartridge to fail at 270 Nm and the 1/2 in hex output at 250 Nm**, and warns that shock loads break parts even within steady ratings ([REV](https://www.revrobotics.com/rev-21-2100/)). By inference, 16:1 at 80 A is about 25 Nm (10x margin) and a stall about 113 Nm. The old 64:1 at 160 A setup was near 80% of the hex rating, so the October regear was the right call.

Heat is the slow risk. Copper loss scales with current squared, so a jam held at the 80 A limit heats the spindexer motor about four times faster than 40 A (inference). Falcon-era Talon FX firmware **shut down at 110 C and restarted at 90 C**, and at a constant 480 W load reached shutdown in **0:59 to 4:41** ([Stryke Force report](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/uploads/short-url/eVYO5tVOYZecwq6Tl2kURlFZFgq.pdf)). CTRE does not publish a Kraken cutoff. Phoenix 6 exposes `getDeviceTemp()` and `getFault_DeviceTemp()` for a software guard ([CTRE API](https://api.ctr-electronics.com/phoenix6/stable/cpp/classctre_1_1phoenix6_1_1hardware_1_1core_1_1_core_talon_f_x.html)).

## Prioritized recommendation with parameter values

The repo already has most of the protection layers. The changes below reorder the ladder and close its gaps. Sourced anchors are linked; every specific number not linked is an engineering inference for the team to validate with DogLog and PDH logs on a used battery.

| Priority | Change | Values | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Gate the starve timer | Count only while ShootCommand is READY and feeding forward; reset on each RPM-dip ball | Inference (avoids false pumps during aim pauses) |
| 2 | Shorten the starve trigger | 0.8 s to **0.4 s** (about 3 ball intervals at 8 BPS; floor 0.3 s) | Inference; 581 uses a 0.25 s debounce on its jam sensor ([581](https://github.com/team581/frc-2026)) |
| 3 | Preventive roller | Intake roller **forward about 5 V** for the whole time ShootCommand runs | Sourced: 581 SHOOT = 5 V ([581](https://github.com/team581/frc-2026)) |
| 4 | Preventive squeeze, stationary shots only | After **0.7 s** of firing, hold the pivot at a partial-lift compaction position (start at DOWN + 1.5 rot, about -4.2; tune); stay at DOWN when shooting on the move | 0.7 s delay sourced from 581; position is inference; "not during shoot-on-the-move" sourced ([CD](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/spindexer-problem/517335)) |
| 5 | Reactive level 1 (first gap) | Roller **out at -9 V for 0.15 s**, then back in, while spindexer and uptake **keep feeding forward**; if the intake is stowed, spindexer 0.15 s reverse pulse instead | Pattern sourced from 581 auto-unjam (eject at the hopper, feed forward); durations inference |
| 6 | Reactive level 2 (second gap) | Existing pump: lift 2.0 rot, 2 cycles, with a **softer pump profile: accel 80 rot/s², jerk 800 rot/s³**, leg timeout 0.45 s to **0.55 s**; drop leg always Motion Magic to DOWN + 0.1 | Inference from Thrifty impact risk; profile timing about 0.4 s per 2 rot leg, check on robot |
| 7 | Keep stall reverse independent | >45 A, <8 rot/s, 0.15 s, then -6 V for 0.25 s; add a spindexer **voltage ramp 0.05-0.1 s**; after 3 unjams in a row, **3 s lockout** | Current-spike reverse sourced ([CD](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/spindexer-problem/517335)); ramp and lockout inference |
| 8 | Stop rule | Keep "3 tries with no ball, stop"; add a rolling cap of **12 pumps per 60 s** | Inference |
| 9 | Thermal guard | Read `getDeviceTemp()` at 4 Hz on the pivot pair, spindexer and roller: **warn 70 C, disable agitation 80 C, re-enable below 65 C**; `Fault_DeviceTemp` = off for the match | API sourced ([CTRE](https://api.ctr-electronics.com/phoenix6/stable/cpp/classctre_1_1phoenix6_1_1hardware_1_1core_1_1_core_talon_f_x.html)); thresholds inference below the Falcon 110 C cutoff |
| 10 | Battery gate | Skip all agitation below **8.5 V** battery or any motor `Fault_Undervoltage` | Inference with headroom over the 6.75 V brownout ([WPILib](https://docs.wpilib.org/es/stable/docs/software/roborio-info/roborio-brownouts.html)) |
| 11 | Current limits | Pivot stays at 40 A stator, never above 60-70 A; spindexer stays at 80 A stator | Thrifty 25 Nm reversal test and REV ratings; margins computed by inference |
| 12 | Drivetrain shuffle (optional, flag default OFF) | Only after level 2 fails, robot stationary, no pump running, flywheel at speed; robot-centric **X axis only, 0.3 m/s, 25 rad/s (about 4 Hz), 0.4 s burst**; **lead compensation off** during the burst; drive stator 60-80 A and supply 20-25 A during the burst, restored after; `end()` commands zero velocity; **no rotation wiggle ever** | Shuffle form sourced from the CD `Tweak` sketch ([CD](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/shake-the-robot/516274)); all limits inference |

The ordering matters more than any single value. Priorities 1-3 cost nothing mechanically and remove most of the lost time. Priority 4 is the one change that targets arch formation rather than arch breaking, and it needs a quick test to find a compaction angle that pushes balls toward the spindexer without lifting the roller off the floor. Priority 12 should be built only if logs show jams that survive level 2 while the robot is parked, and it should be judged on "Shots At Speed" and BPS logs, not on feel.

## Conclusion

Asking "should we shake the drivetrain" assumes the problem is reaction speed. The evidence says it is mostly prevention and geometry. A turret robot that refuses to fire unless the turret is nearly still has made the drivetrain the worst possible agitator, because every useful drive motion is exactly what the READY gate exists to reject. The team's real lever is the pivot it already owns: used as a slow, held squeeze instead of a reactive pump, it does what 581 does. Even the best software ladder only shortens gaps. Zero gaps needs an inlet that admits one ball at a time, so if logs keep showing level-2 jams at the same spot, the next fix belongs in CAD (a star wheel or a narrower inlet at the bridge), not in more agitation code.

The main uncertainties are three. No team has published BPS with and without a given agitation scheme. Kraken thermal cutoffs are unpublished. 5669's actual jam location and recovery time have not been measured. The first practice session with the new ladder should log gap durations, pump counts, motor temperatures and battery minimums, so the values above can be replaced with measured ones.
