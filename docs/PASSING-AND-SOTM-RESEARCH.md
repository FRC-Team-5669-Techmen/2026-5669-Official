# Elite REBUILT Code Validates 5669's Passing Plan

Team 5669's passing and shoot-on-the-move design already matches what the best-documented elite 2026 codebases shipped. Its primary corner target (0.94 m off the wall, 1.65 m off the side) is exactly 6328's. Its backup (1.40/1.00) is 581's backup. Its 1.5 m/s scoring and 4.0 m/s passing caps are 581's numbers, and its drag constant k = 0.42 sits inside the 0.375 to 0.465 band that 6328 and 581 tuned independently. The work left is a short list of specific gaps, not a redesign. First, a fixed "turret slower than 30 deg/s" READY gate blocks legitimate moving shots, because the turret has to rotate to track the hub while the robot strafes. Second, the G407 score line has to be checked at the predicted release pose, not the decision pose: at 1.5 m/s with a 0.25 s feed delay the robot travels 0.375 m, more than the 0.15 m margin in the 4.19 m line. Third, the hub-close grace has to be counted to ball arrival, because the manual keeps counting only 3 s after deactivation. Fourth, there is a wedge directly behind our hub, about 1.6 m deep, where both the primary and backup corner lines cross the padded hub. Fifth, the passing table has to cover about 3.5 to 13 m, because motion at 4 m/s shrinks or stretches the virtual pass distance by roughly 4 m. On the rules side, passing from the neutral zone into your own alliance zone is legal and is never gated by hub state, and shooting while climbing is legal because the TOWER sits inside the alliance zone. The real constraints are G407 (launch into your own hub only with bumpers in your zone), G405 (no intentional out-of-field FUEL), G404 (never at opponents), and the 30 in / 12 in expansion limits.

## Three elite codebases pass to the corners 5669 already picked

The strongest evidence is published 2026 robot code from 6328, 581 and 1678. Each has a dedicated passing mode that aims at **fixed floor points deep in its own alliance zone, never at the hub**, and picks a side from the robot's position. 6328 aims at a point **37 in (0.94 m) from the alliance wall and 65 in (1.65 m) from the side wall**, mirrored to the far side when the robot is past field center ([6328 LaunchCalculator.java](https://github.com/Mechanical-Advantage/RobotCode2026Public/blob/main/src/main/java/org/littletonrobotics/frc2026/subsystems/launcher/LaunchCalculator.java)). 581 aims at (15.75, 3.0) and (15.75, fieldWidth − 3.0) for red. When `isFeedPathObstructed` finds the straight line blocked by either hub (each padded by **12 in**), it falls back to (15.15, 1.0) or (15.15, 7.1) ([581 FieldUtil.java](https://github.com/team581/frc-2026/blob/main/shared/src/main/java/com/team581/util/FieldUtil.java), [581 FeedLocation.java](https://github.com/team581/frc-2026/blob/main/shared/src/main/java/com/team581/util/FeedLocation.java)). Inference: on the 16.541 m welded field, 581's backup works out to 1.39 m off the wall and 1.0 m off the side, the same point as 5669's backup. 1678 uses a different shape. Its six ferry states put deep targets 0.5 m off the wall at y = 2.44 m and 4.72 m (inference: on either side of the TOWER, not in the corners), move to shallower side targets (y 1.52 and 6.10 m) when the robot is inside the opponent's zone, and drop to a neutral-zone target at x = 5.5 m when it is within 6.5 ft of the opponent wall ([1678 Targeting.java](https://github.com/frc1678/C2026-Public/blob/main/src/main/java/frc/robot/shooting/targeting/Targeting.java), [1678 FieldLayout.java](https://github.com/frc1678/C2026-Public/blob/main/src/main/java/frc/lib/util/FieldLayout.java)).

All three switch between score and pass from pose alone, and they draw the line in slightly different places: 6328 at its own hub center x, 581 at the hub/bump obstacle line, and 1678 at the alliance line + 4 ft ([6328 LaunchCalculator.java](https://github.com/Mechanical-Advantage/RobotCode2026Public/blob/main/src/main/java/org/littletonrobotics/frc2026/subsystems/launcher/LaunchCalculator.java), [581 RobotManager.java](https://github.com/team581/frc-2026/blob/main/comp-bot/src/main/java/frc/robot/robot_manager/RobotManager.java)). They also disagree on target stability. 581 re-picks the feed target every loop, while **1678 freezes the target while firing** ("avoid target changing") ([1678 Targeting.java](https://github.com/frc1678/C2026-Public/blob/main/src/main/java/frc/robot/shooting/targeting/Targeting.java)). 6328 handles obstacles differently from 581. Instead of a line-of-sight check, it refuses to launch inside "boxes of bad": under the tower, behind the far hub, and a `nearHubBound` box running from our hub's neutral face to the center line across the whole bump band. That box is roughly x 5.22 to 8.27 m and y 1.58 to 6.49 m, so in practice 6328 passes from the near half only through the trench lanes ([6328 LaunchCalculator.java](https://github.com/Mechanical-Advantage/RobotCode2026Public/blob/main/src/main/java/org/littletonrobotics/frc2026/subsystems/launcher/LaunchCalculator.java)). The code does not say why the box is that large.

**Inference, computed for this report:** 5669 already uses 581's approach (a line-of-sight check with a fallback to the backup target), and that leaves one region where the fallback fails too. Taking robot center as the launch point and padding the hub by 12 in, both the primary and backup lines cross our hub from roughly **x 5.4 to 7.0 m (blue frame)**. The blocked band is about y 3.25 to 4.75 m at the hub face and narrows to about y 3.75 to 4.25 m at x 7.0 m. From (8.0, 4.0) the primary line just clips the padding, but the backup clears. Inside that wedge the robot should hold fire and tell the driver to slide sideways, not lob over the hub. A pass launched from outside the zone that drops into our own hub is, word for word, a G407 launch into our HUB. The rear net stops "most" such shots, not all of them ([2026 Game Manual](https://firstfrc.blob.core.windows.net/frc2026/Manual/2026GameManual.pdf)).

Passing tables are separate from hub tables everywhere, and the passing gates are looser everywhere. 6328 **fixes the hood at 38 deg (its maximum) for every pass** and changes range with flywheel speed only: 160 rad/s at 5.46 m up to 400 rad/s at 17.16 m, with time of flight rising from 1.27 to 2.5 s. 1678 lowers its hood from 52.5 to 43 deg as range grows from 5 to 10 m, at 1800 to 2650 RPM. 581 runs 1500 to 2500 RPM over 6 to 13.6 m ([6328 LaunchCalculator.java](https://github.com/Mechanical-Advantage/RobotCode2026Public/blob/main/src/main/java/org/littletonrobotics/frc2026/subsystems/launcher/LaunchCalculator.java), [1678 ShooterVelocityRegressions.java](https://github.com/frc1678/C2026-Public/blob/main/src/main/java/frc/robot/shooting/regressions/ShooterVelocityRegressions.java), [581 ShooterConfig.java](https://github.com/team581/frc-2026/blob/main/comp-bot/src/main/java/frc/robot/shooter/ShooterConfig.java)). 581 accepts **20 in of aim error at the target when feeding versus 5 in when scoring**, and **500 RPM** of flywheel error. 6328 accepts about 382 RPM (40 rad/s) and 15 deg of yaw ([581 AimParameterUtil.java](https://github.com/team581/frc-2026/blob/main/comp-bot/src/main/java/frc/robot/util/AimParameterUtil.java), [6328 RobotContainer.java](https://github.com/Mechanical-Advantage/RobotCode2026Public/blob/main/src/main/java/org/littletonrobotics/frc2026/RobotContainer.java)). These RPMs do not carry over to 5669's wheel. What carries over is the pattern: hood high, tune RPM, record time of flight.

Elite teams pass on the run, while intaking, and regardless of hub state. 581 has a `FEED_AND_INTAKE` hopper state, caps translation at **4.0 m/s while feeding (of 4.75 m/s max)**, and runs its motion lead with a separate feed time-of-flight table ([581 HopperState.java](https://github.com/team581/frc-2026/blob/main/comp-bot/src/main/java/frc/robot/robot_manager/hopper_manager/HopperState.java), [581 Swerve.java](https://github.com/team581/frc-2026/blob/main/comp-bot/src/main/java/frc/robot/swerve/Swerve.java)). 6328's one-button launch fires when `hubActiveOrPassing` is true, so passes are never held for a shift. 581's FEED state has no hub check at all ([6328 RobotContainer.java](https://github.com/Mechanical-Advantage/RobotCode2026Public/blob/main/src/main/java/org/littletonrobotics/frc2026/RobotContainer.java), [581 RobotManager.java](https://github.com/team581/frc-2026/blob/main/comp-bot/src/main/java/frc/robot/robot_manager/RobotManager.java)). Both keep a fallback for when the pose is bad. 581's `FALLBACK_FEED` points the robot straight at its own wall, assumes 8.0 m, and accepts ±5 deg ([581 AimParameterUtil.java](https://github.com/team581/frc-2026/blob/main/comp-bot/src/main/java/frc/robot/util/AimParameterUtil.java)). 6328 puts a fixed passing preset (5.5 m hub-map values) on a driver paddle ([6328 RobotContainer.java](https://github.com/Mechanical-Advantage/RobotCode2026Public/blob/main/src/main/java/org/littletonrobotics/frc2026/RobotContainer.java)). On strategy, pre-season Chief Delphi argued for passing home during the opponent's active shift so you can unload when your own hub turns on. The counterargument was that at high levels, three robots carrying FUEL themselves may beat passing, because re-intaking passed balls is slow ([CD: Will ferrying or passing dominate](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/will-ferrying-or-passing-dominate-rebuilt/511443)). No source gave measured passing balls per second, landing scatter, or the passing code of 254, 1323, 4414 or 2910.

## Moving shots lead by about 80 percent of flight time

Every public 2026 implementation **iterates a virtual target**: look up time of flight at the compensated distance, shift the goal, repeat. 581 and 5000 run 5 iterations and 6328 runs 20. 1706's 2022 code stopped once the change fell under 10 ms, capped at 5 passes ([581 ShootOnTheMove.java](https://github.com/team581/frc-2026/blob/HEAD/shared/src/main/java/com/team581/math/ShootOnTheMove.java), [5000 TurretCalculator.java](https://github.com/Hammerheads5000/2026Rebuilt/blob/HEAD/src/main/java/frc/robot/subsystems/turret/TurretCalculator.java), [CD 1706 thread](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/shoot-while-move-code-1706/410494?page=2)). The 2026-specific addition is drag. Both 6328 (k = 0.375) and 581 (k = 0.465, "quickly tuned") lead by an **effective time of flight (1 − e^(−k·t))/k** rather than the raw table value, because the horizontal velocity the foam FUEL inherits from the robot decays in flight ([6328 LaunchCalculator.java](https://github.com/Mechanical-Advantage/RobotCode2026Public/blob/HEAD/src/main/java/org/littletonrobotics/frc2026/subsystems/launcher/LaunchCalculator.java), [581 ShootOnTheMove.java](https://github.com/team581/frc-2026/blob/HEAD/shared/src/main/java/com/team581/math/ShootOnTheMove.java)). Inference: at 5669's k = 0.42, the lead is 82% of raw time of flight at 1.0 s and 78% at 1.25 s, which agrees with both teams. RPM and hood come from the stationary table indexed by the **virtual** distance. 254 (2022), 1706, 581, 6328 and 5000 all do it this way, and 254's closed form shows why: the ball needs range/t − v_radial of its own speed ([254 ShootingUtil.java](https://github.com/Team254/FRC-2022-Public/blob/HEAD/src/main/java/com/team254/frc2022/shooting/ShootingUtil.java)).

The velocity input is split. 581, 5000 and 254 use measured field-relative chassis speeds. 6328 uses the commanded setpoint in teleop and extrapolates the pose by a **0.03 s phase delay** before any shot math ([581 Swerve.java](https://github.com/team581/frc-2026/blob/HEAD/comp-bot/src/main/java/frc/robot/swerve/Swerve.java), [6328 RobotState.java](https://github.com/Mechanical-Advantage/RobotCode2026Public/blob/HEAD/src/main/java/org/littletonrobotics/frc2026/RobotState.java)). 581 and 6328 both add the shooter's swing velocity from rotation (ω × r) and solve from the shooter's field position. Separately, 581 measured **0.25 s of feeder-to-shooter travel** from match logs and uses it as a lookahead in its ready check ([581 ShooterConfig.java](https://github.com/team581/frc-2026/blob/HEAD/comp-bot/src/main/java/frc/robot/shooter/ShooterConfig.java)). Inference: 5669's turret pivot is 0.164 m off center, so at the 2.5 rad/s vision-reject spin rate the swing term alone is about 0.41 m/s, which is as large as a slow strafe. The team's existing pivot-velocity term matters.

The standard turret feedforward is **ω_ff = −ω_robot + v_tangential/d**: 254 in 2022, and 5000, the closest public turret analog in 2026. 5000 adds a tangential-acceleration term and sends the result as a Phoenix 6 position request with `.withVelocity()` ([254 ShootingUtil.java](https://github.com/Team254/FRC-2022-Public/blob/HEAD/src/main/java/com/team254/frc2022/shooting/ShootingUtil.java), [5000 Turret.java](https://github.com/Hammerheads5000/2026Rebuilt/blob/HEAD/src/main/java/frc/robot/subsystems/turret/Turret.java)). 6328 differentiates its final aim angle numerically and smooths it with a 0.1 s moving average, which captures the virtual-target motion automatically ([6328 LaunchCalculator.java](https://github.com/Mechanical-Advantage/RobotCode2026Public/blob/HEAD/src/main/java/org/littletonrobotics/frc2026/subsystems/launcher/LaunchCalculator.java)). On readiness, 581 converts a linear tolerance at the goal into an angle, **atan(5 in / d)** for scoring and atan(20 in / d) for feeding. 6328 uses fixed yaw, pitch and roll tolerances. 254 marked a shot invalid if the motion correction exceeded 45 deg or the turret hit a limit ([581 AimParameterUtil.java](https://github.com/team581/frc-2026/blob/HEAD/comp-bot/src/main/java/frc/robot/util/AimParameterUtil.java), [254 Constants.java](https://github.com/Team254/FRC-2022-Public/blob/HEAD/src/main/java/com/team254/frc2022/Constants.java)). On speed, 581 caps scoring at 1.5 m/s and 4 rot/s. 6328 caps the robot's orbit rate around the hub at 0.4 rad/s, which works out to about 1.2 m/s of pure strafe at 3 m ([6328 DriveCommands.java](https://github.com/Mechanical-Advantage/RobotCode2026Public/blob/HEAD/src/main/java/org/littletonrobotics/frc2026/commands/DriveCommands.java)). No team published make-rate versus speed.

**Inference, and the most important code finding for 5669:** the project notes still describe READY as "turret within 2 deg and slower than 30 deg/s." A robot strafing at the 1.5 m/s cap 2 m from the hub needs the turret turning at v_t/d = 0.75 rad/s, or **43 deg/s**, before any spin cancellation. The fixed speed gate therefore closes exactly when the motion lead is doing its job. Replace it with "turret velocity error against the feedforward setpoint is small," as 5000 logs `Turret/VelError`. Passing also changes the geometry a lot. At 4 m/s with a raw time of flight near 1.45 s, the drag-corrected lead is about 4 m. Driving home cuts a 7.7 m center-line pass to a virtual distance near 4 m, and driving away stretches it toward 12 to 13 m. A passing table that covers only the static 5 to 12 m distances will clamp at both ends.

## REBUILT polices the launch, not where passes land

The only location rule on launching is **G407**: a robot "may not launch a SCORING ELEMENT into their HUB unless their BUMPERS are partially or fully within their ALLIANCE ZONE," a **MAJOR FOUL worth 15 points** to the opponent. Nothing forbids launching from the neutral zone into your own alliance zone, and robots may hold any number of FUEL ([2026 Game Manual TU22](https://firstfrc.blob.core.windows.net/frc2026/Manual/2026GameManual.pdf)). The other limits on passing are G404 (no launching at opponent robots, MAJOR), G405 (no intentionally ejecting FUEL from the field, MINOR, MAJOR if repeated in the same match), G406 (no damaging FUEL) and G408 (no catching or redirecting FUEL leaving the hub, including "sitting under the HUB to collect") ([Manual 7.4.2](https://firstfrc.blob.core.windows.net/frc2026/Manual/2026GameManual.pdf)). The long-side guardrails are only **20 in tall** and the alliance-wall glass tops out near 2.0 m, so landing points well inside the field are the real safeguard ([Manual 5.2](https://firstfrc.blob.core.windows.net/frc2026/Manual/2026GameManual.pdf)). Chief Delphi posters treated passing home, even off your own driver-station glass, as normal play, but warned that repeated high misses could read as intentional ejection ([CD: Passing Into Driver Glass](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/passing-into-driver-glass/513795)). No official Q&A on passing was readable.

The alliance zone is **158.6 in (4.029 m) deep** from the wall. Hub centers are (4.6255, 4.0345) blue and (11.9155, 4.0345) red, which matches 5669's `Constants.Aim` ([Manual 5.3](https://firstfrc.blob.core.windows.net/frc2026/Manual/2026GameManual.pdf), [Field Dimension Drawings](https://firstfrc.blob.core.windows.net/frc2026/FieldAssets/2026-field-dimension-dwgs.pdf)). Inference: 5669's smallest bumper half-extent is 0.311 m, so the heading-independent legal line is robot center ≤ 4.34 m from the wall. The team's 4.19 m line is that minus a 0.15 m pose margin. The rule is judged at launch, though, and the ball leaves after the feed delay. At 1.5 m/s and 581's 0.25 s feed time, a robot backing out of the zone travels 0.375 m between the decision and the release. The check should run against x + v_x·t_feed, with roughly 0.10 m of hysteresis.

Hub shifts follow a fixed schedule: AUTO 20 s, then TELEOP with a 10 s TRANSITION, four 25 s SHIFTS (timer 2:10, 1:45, 1:20, 0:55, 0:30) and a 30 s END GAME. Both hubs are active in AUTO, TRANSITION and END GAME. The alliance that scored more FUEL in AUTO goes **inactive in SHIFTS 1 and 3**, and game data ('R' or 'B') names that alliance about 3 s after AUTO ([Manual 6.4](https://firstfrc.blob.core.windows.net/frc2026/Manual/2026GameManual.pdf), [WPILib 2026 Game Data](https://docs.wpilib.org/en/latest/docs/yearly-overview/2026-game-data.html)). WPILib's examples treat empty game data as "active," and FMS sends robots only an approximate match time. The key timing rule is that "assessment of FUEL scored in the HUB continues for **3 seconds after the HUB deactivates** to account for FUEL processing time," and a FUEL counts once it passes the sensor array ([Manual 6.5](https://firstfrc.blob.core.windows.net/frc2026/Manual/2026GameManual.pdf)). 581 gates scoring on whether the hub will be active when the ball **arrives** (match time + time of flight + a tunable offset). 6328 adds fudges of −(minTOF + 1.0 s) on approach and 3.0 − (maxTOF + 2.0 s) at the close, and re-syncs to DS match time when its own timer drifts more than 3 s ([581 HubActivity.java](https://github.com/team581/frc-2026/blob/main/comp-bot/src/main/java/frc/robot/hub_activity/HubActivity.java), [6328 HubShiftUtil.java](https://github.com/Mechanical-Advantage/RobotCode2026Public/blob/main/src/main/java/org/littletonrobotics/frc2026/util/HubShiftUtil.java)). Inference: if that close fudge is applied to the deactivation time, 6328's last release comes about 0.25 s before deactivation at a 1.25 s maximum time of flight, which leaves about 2 s of the 3 s window unused as margin. If 5669's 2.5 s close grace lets the robot *release* up to 2.5 s after deactivation, balls arrive about 3.6 to 3.9 s after it and score nothing. If the grace means "ball arrives by deactivation + 2.5 s," the release cutoff is deactivation + 2.5 − TOF(d). That is legal, with a thinner margin than 6328's.

Shooting while climbing is legal, because no rule prohibits it and the TOWER sits inside the alliance zone. The blue tower base runs x 0 to 1.148 m, centered near y 3.745 m ([Manual 5.8, 6.5.2](https://firstfrc.blob.core.windows.net/frc2026/Manual/2026GameManual.pdf)). Inference: that puts a robot on the tower 3.4 to 4.0 m from the hub center, at the far end of 5669's recorded table. The binding constraints are **R107's 30 in height limit, measured perpendicular to the frame** (so tilting on the tower does not excuse an extended hood), R105/R106's 12 in, one-side extension limit (enforced by G413 as a MAJOR FOUL, and applicable when full of FUEL per TU13), and G412, which allows grabbing only RUNGS and UPRIGHTS ([Manual 8.1, 7.4.3](https://firstfrc.blob.core.windows.net/frc2026/Manual/2026GameManual.pdf), [Team Updates](https://firstfrc.blob.core.windows.net/frc2026/Manual/TeamUpdates/REBUILT_TeamUpdate-Combined.pdf)). Two more rules matter for driving and autos. The space under each TRENCH arm is **22.25 in tall**, and TU22's final G403 bans completely crossing the CENTER LINE in AUTO ([Manual 5.6, 7.4.1](https://firstfrc.blob.core.windows.net/frc2026/Manual/2026GameManual.pdf)). Inference: 5669's Limelight lens reaches 22.35 in with the hood up, so a hood raised for a pass will hit the trench arm.

Tune k with a controlled strafe test. Drive past the hub at a steady 1 m/s inside the 1.5 m/s cap. If balls land ahead of the hub in the direction of travel, the lead is too small, so lower k (a smaller k lengthens the effective time of flight). If they land behind it, the lead is too large, so raise k. (Inference: the source notes stated this rule with the direction reversed; the sign follows from virtual target = hub − v·t_eff.) Smooth the time-of-flight tables to monotonic before relying on them. The published 2026 tables are noisy (581's 2.42 m point is longer than its 3.54 m point), and a non-monotonic table makes the iteration chatter ([581 ShooterConfig.java](https://github.com/team581/frc-2026/blob/HEAD/comp-bot/src/main/java/frc/robot/shooter/ShooterConfig.java)). Two unknowns remain. No Beach Blitz rule changes were found, so the default is the final TU22 manual. And passing while intaking depends on the roller, Talon FX 22, which took the CAN bus down on Oct 26 and is currently unplugged.

## Conclusion

The evidence supports keeping 5669's architecture, with one caveat about what that support means. 5669's targets and speed caps are borrowed from 6328 and 581, so agreeing with them proves the numbers are plausible, not that they suit this robot. The stronger signal is the drag constant: 6328 and 581 tuned k independently and landed within 25% of each other, which suggests the roughly 80% lead factor comes from the FUEL itself and carries over to other robots. The remaining errors are in the gates and the timing: a turret-speed check that conflicts with the feedforward it sits beside, a zone line checked a quarter-second too early, a close grace measured to the wrong event, and one pocket behind the hub where every pass line is blocked. Each of these makes the robot either hold fire when it could legally shoot, or fire when the ball will not count or will draw a 15-point foul, so fixing them pays off directly in points. The open question the sources cannot answer is accuracy.
