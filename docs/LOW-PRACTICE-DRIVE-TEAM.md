# Let the robot carry the drive team

Team 5669's fastest way to compete well at Beach Blitz (Oct 30 to Nov 1, 2026) with an underpracticed drive team is to make the robot do the jobs that practice normally teaches, then shrink the human job to a short, scripted plan. Most of the robot side is already done. Commit 6c6b4b5 on main turns auto-aim on at teleop start, fixes a stick that was dead until about 37% throw, slows the robot to the fire limit and X-locks the wheels while scoring, gives every mode change a buzz the drive team can feel, and closes the button traps a code audit found (RT feeding into a stopped wheel, RT dead after B, stray pose resets). What remains is people work. Train with the team's own code in simulation and a few targeted drills. Measure two times, "come home" and "climb now", that set the coach's two biggest calls. Run one fixed game plan: score from inside our zone during our shifts, go through the trench to collect and pass during theirs, then hang and keep shooting in endgame. Use the Oct 30 practice matches as the robot time the team never had. The best 2026 teams dropped several driver assists "after practice" ([581 code release](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/521762/2)), which is the strongest evidence that those same assists are worth the most to a team that has not practiced. The risk is the opposite one: every assist changes what the drivers have rehearsed, so nothing new should ship after the practice matches.

## Do this before Beach Blitz, in this order

Each step is ordered by points protected per minute spent. Steps 1 to 4 need the robot for about an hour in total. The rest need only laptops, paper and people.

## The robot now does what an unpracticed operator forgets

The code audit of Oct 27 ranked the likeliest drive-team mistakes by how often each comes up and how silently it fails. The top item was that nothing turned on at teleop start. The operator had to remember Operator RB every match, and the turret stopped watching the hub the moment auto ended, so vision fixes also stopped (team code audit, commit e9b18d9). The elite pattern is the opposite. 6328 deploys its intake and zeroes mechanisms automatically at teleop start ([6328 RobotContainer](https://github.com/Mechanical-Advantage/RobotCode2026Public/blob/559fa65be991303fec81d03c3de21e82a219da7c/src/main/java/org/littletonrobotics/frc2026/RobotContainer.java#L1075)). 581 puts score-or-feed on a single trigger and lets the robot decide which by field position ([581 Robot.java](https://github.com/team581/frc-2026/blob/5037ac428146c253d1648bcde6d95bbf15d0d2ef/comp-bot/src/main/java/frc/robot/Robot.java#L175)). 5669 already picked its target by position (score with the robot center within **4.19 m / 165 in** of our wall, pass beyond that). What it lacked was the default. These changes are built and on main:

| Built (6c6b4b5) | What the drive team notices | Off switch |
|---|---|---|
| **Auto-aim on at teleop start** and when defense ends. With no shot wanted, only the turret tracks, the hood stays down (trench-safe) and the robot never turns itself. | The turret follows the hub from the first second. Nobody has to remember RB. | Dashboard "Auto-Aim On At Teleop Start" |
| **Stick dead zone on the stick** (8%, rescaled) before the cubic curve. | The old deadband sat on the output velocity, so the stick did nothing until about **37% throw (46% in slow mode)**, then lurched to 0.22 m/s. Now small moves move the robot. | None (feel change) |
| **Slow mode also on Driver RB** (hold) | Slow down without taking the right thumb off the rotate stick. | Start still works |
| **0.5 s hold, then a buzz**, for Driver LB gyro reset and Operator A/X/LB backup shots | A brushed button no longer resets the heading or moves the pose to a backup spot. | None |
| **Scoring speed cap and X-lock**: with RT held while scoring, drive is capped at 1.35 m/s, just under the 1.5 m/s fire limit. Sticks centered and robot slow puts the wheels in an X. | The robot keeps firing while the driver creeps instead of silently pausing, and a bump doesn't knock it off aim. Driver left stick click overrides. | Dashboard "Slow Down While Shooting" |
| **RT with auto-aim off spins up and waits for speed**. RT resumes after B (unjam). | No more balls fed into a stopped wheel, and no dead RT after clearing a jam. | None |
| **Haptic vocabulary** (table below) | Every mode change can be felt. | None |
| Dump lowers the intake itself. No intake pumps during vacuum. The intake jog keeps Driver A in sync. Hood Y picks the farther preset. Shooter idle resets at match start. | Fewer moves that need two people. Fewer "press it twice" moments. | None |
| **Pit Check** flags robot-centric drive, speed limit under 100%, idle left on, and saved switches off their match settings. | Leftovers from the shop are caught before the match. | None |
| New big **"Drive Team"** Elastic tab (Ready To Fire, Aim Active, Pose Trusted, Our Hub, Shot Ready, Shot Target, Vacuum, Dump, Defense, Pit Check), plus Match tab additions | One screen shows every latched mode. | None |

The elite record supports each piece. 581 caps linear speed at **1.5 m/s while scoring** and 4.0 m/s while feeding ([581 Swerve.java](https://github.com/team581/frc-2026/blob/5037ac428146c253d1648bcde6d95bbf15d0d2ef/comp-bot/src/main/java/frc/robot/swerve/Swerve.java#L61)). 5000 drops to a slow drive profile whenever it scores ([5000 Constants](https://github.com/Hammerheads5000/2026Rebuilt/blob/279745f273441470f86b78dece9d9c8653db0e2c/src/main/java/frc/robot/Constants.java#L123)). 581 X-locks after a 0.1 s debounce once aimed and stopped ([581 Swerve.java](https://github.com/team581/frc-2026/blob/5037ac428146c253d1648bcde6d95bbf15d0d2ef/comp-bot/src/main/java/frc/robot/swerve/Swerve.java#L381)), and 6328 does the same with an "O-lock" below 0.1 m/s ([6328 DriveCommands](https://github.com/Mechanical-Advantage/RobotCode2026Public/blob/559fa65be991303fec81d03c3de21e82a219da7c/src/main/java/org/littletonrobotics/frc2026/commands/DriveCommands.java#L295)). Every elite team reviewed deadbands the raw stick, between 0.05 and 0.225, and rescales it. CTRE's own request deadband is a hard cut on the commanded velocity ([CTRE SwerveRequest source](https://api.ctr-electronics.com/phoenix6/stable/cpp/_swerve_request_8hpp_source.html)), which is why 5669's old setting made the stick feel dead and then lurch.

The buzz vocabulary follows the human-factors evidence. Mode errors come from hidden machine states and automatic transitions that the interface doesn't show ([Andre & Degani](https://www3.fmb.unesp.br/sete2021/pluginfile.php/20273/mod_assign/intro/Doyouknowwhatmode.pdf)). Vibration in a handheld controller produced faster "stop" reactions than sound alone ([Markiewicz et al. 2024](https://research.utwente.nl/en/publications/you-got-it-in-your-hands-stop-signal-modality-influences-on-react/)), and players found it easier to detect than audio, though some could not tell which side buzzed ([Charbonneau et al.](https://www.eecs.ucf.edu/~jjl/pubs/p79-charbonneau.pdf)). So the patterns differ by count and length, not by side.

| Feel | Means |
|---|---|
| 1 short | Something turned ON (aim on, gyro reset done, backup spot set) |
| 2 short | Something turned OFF (including defense ending by itself) |
| 1 long | Vacuum or dump started: the robot will move the intake |
| 1 / 2 / 3 short (Operator Start) | Target mode AUTO / SCORE only / PASS only |
| 3 long | Hopper looks empty: go get balls |
| On/off pulses on both controllers, 6 to 20 s into teleop | No FMS game data: operator sets "Auto Winner" |
| Fast strong pulses (driver) | Defense pin near 3 s: back off |
| Steady strong (driver) | Last 30 s near **their** tower: get away |
| Ticks in the last 5 s of a shift (driver) | Hubs about to swap |
| Steady medium (operator) | Ready to fire |

### Build next: trench lock first, then climb help; leave deceleration alone

**Trench lock** is the best-supported assist not yet built. 5000's driver said "I could go pretty much full speed through the trench without issue" ([5000 build thread #384](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/507502/384)). The whole thing is about 15 lines. A trigger fires when the pose *predicted 0.5 s ahead* is inside a trench zone. A PID (kP 8) then drives field Y to the trench center line, and a heading PID (kP 5) turns to the nearer of ±90°, while the driver keeps forward and back ([5000 TeleopDrive](https://github.com/Hammerheads5000/2026Rebuilt/blob/279745f273441470f86b78dece9d9c8653db0e2c/src/main/java/frc/robot/commands/TeleopDrive.java#L214)). 581 built a softer version that blends at most 75% into the driver's input, then listed it as deprecated once its practiced driver no longer needed it ([581 SwerveAssist](https://github.com/team581/frc-2026/blob/5037ac428146c253d1648bcde6d95bbf15d0d2ef/shared/src/main/java/com/team581/swerve/SwerveAssist.java#L181)). The opening is only **50.34 in (1.28 m) wide**, and 5669's bumpers are 29.5 x 24.5 in, so pick the heading that leaves the most side clearance. Use the trench band the hood guard already knows, require Pose Trusted, and let the rotate stick cancel it. It cannot be tested properly on the shop field, whose trench tag is not at the official position. Ship it before Beach Blitz only as a **held** button (Driver X is free). Make it automatic after the event.

**Climb help** comes second. 6328 holds the heading at the nearer of ±90° while the driver keeps translation, for lining up under the tower ([6328 DriveCommands](https://github.com/Mechanical-Advantage/RobotCode2026Public/blob/559fa65be991303fec81d03c3de21e82a219da7c/src/main/java/org/littletonrobotics/frc2026/commands/DriveCommands.java#L145)). 1678 puts the whole climb setup on one button: intake to a safe spot, hood stowed, shooter idle, climber to max ([1678 ControlBoard](https://github.com/frc1678/C2026-Public/blob/0f403dd8f3ad1f7d0dbe0c1bd23b24c210f41677/src/main/java/frc/robot/controlboard/ControlBoard.java#L225)). For 5669, a "prep climb" macro should leave auto-aim on, since the robot shoots while hanging. Both are hours of work, but the climb drill in the checklist should come first: if the drill shows the driver lining up fast, neither is urgent.

**Deceleration**: leave it. The drive slew limit is 3/s on the shaped stick, so a full-speed release ramps 4.39 m/s to zero in 0.33 s. That asks for about **13 m/s²**, and the robot can deliver only about 10.8 m/s² of grip at the wheel friction 1.1 that PathPlanner assumes, and about 12.8 m/s² from the 70 A slip current (CLAUDE.md figures, not measured on this carpet). The current stop is already at the traction limit. A faster limiter would mostly skid the wheels, and skidding wheels also corrupt odometry, which shoot-from-anywhere depends on. If stops feel long, the cheaper fixes are to release earlier and to use slow mode on RB. Run "Test: Slip Current" to replace the assumed friction with a measured one.

Three smaller items are worth doing after the event. First, buzz until "Auto Winner" is set, as 6328 does ([elite readiness notes, 6328 RobotContainer](https://github.com/Mechanical-Advantage/RobotCode2026Public/blob/559fa65be991303fec81d03c3de21e82a219da7c/src/main/java/org/littletonrobotics/frc2026/RobotContainer.java#L1020)), instead of stopping at 20 s. Second, add a slow operator pulse when "Seconds Since Vision Fix" grows past about 5 s with aim on, because Pose Trusted never decays by itself. Third, add a turret end-of-travel rumble like 5000's 30° zones ([5000 RobotContainer](https://github.com/Hammerheads5000/2026Rebuilt/blob/279745f273441470f86b78dece9d9c8653db0e2c/src/main/java/frc/robot/RobotContainer.java#L382)). Skip object-detection intake steering: 581 found it "didn't provide a benefit" and 1690 judged it "very little value" in teleop ([581 #6](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/521762/6), [1690 #46](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/520673/46)). Skip a bump-snap assist too: 581 dropped theirs because it made bump crossings worse against a defender ([581 #2](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/521762/2)).

## Train with the team's own code, not a game

Two kinds of simulator exist, and they teach different things. xRC and MoSim teach the REBUILT field from the driver station: where the bumps, trenches, hubs and lanes are, and how partners and opponents move. xRC's REBUILT release was "based on the CAD but NOT the game-manual" ([xRC, Jan 2026](https://xrcsimulator.org/rebuilt-released/)), and drivers describe its swerve as slippery and "arcade style" ([CD "XRC Vs. MoSim"](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/xrc-vs-mosim/521308)). Use it for geography, and warn the drivers not to copy its feel. The higher-value sim for 5669 is its own robot code. It already runs `./gradlew simulateJava` with MapleSim physics. Team 5516 built a whole "driving simulator game" on this setup: real robot code, real Xbox controllers in the WPILib Sim GUI, and AdvantageScope's 3D field ([5516 Iron Maple, CD 2024](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/a-crescendo-driving-simulator-game-powered-by-java-robot-code/471345)). Two setup details matter. In the Sim GUI, drag each controller from "System Joysticks" to "Joysticks" and turn on **"Map gamepad"**, because the sim does not copy the Driver Station's Xbox mapping by default ([WPILib Simulation GUI](https://docs.wpilib.org/en/stable/_sources/docs/software/wpilib-tools/robot-simulation/simulation-gui.rst.txt)). In AdvantageScope's 3D Field, the **"Driver Station" camera** puts the view behind a driver station at eye height, and the "Evergreen" fields run on weak laptops ([AdvantageScope 3D Field](https://docs.advantagescope.org/tab-reference/3d-field)). The Joysticks tab shows every press live, so a mentor can watch what a trainee actually hits ([AdvantageScope Joysticks](https://docs.advantagescope.org/tab-reference/joysticks)).

Set expectations honestly. No controlled FRC study of sim-to-robot transfer exists. The best outside evidence, from truck-driver training, shows basic control and procedure transferring to the real vehicle ([Hirsch et al. 2017](https://trid.trb.org/View/1438251)), and FRC opinion splits between "helped driver training" and "can build some pretty bad habits" ([CD 2024](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/how-to-do-driver-practice/472072)). So the own-code sim is a **button-and-mode drill**, not a shooting drill. It rehearses the bindings, the buzzes, defense on and off, vacuum and dump, target mode, the backup shots and the call list. It does not rehearse ball flight or real latency. A good 45-minute session runs the plan card from start to finish twice. The coach calls from the list and the operator answers "what mode are we in?" after random pauses.

With the robot scarce, use it only for drills that the sim cannot cover. FIRST's driver guidance is to start slow and speed up ("Slow is Smooth, Smooth is Fast"), time full cycles on the paths you will actually run, and add "variable" drills: blocked paths, a defender, a mechanism failure ([FIRST "Improving Driving Performance"](https://community.firstinspires.org/hubfs/web/program/frc/resources/improving-driver-performance.pdf)). The classic swerve warm-up is circling a cone with the robot's face always toward it, credited to 1678's driver workshop ([CD 2024](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/how-to-do-driver-practice/472072)). Another is a parking drill: drive into a slot between two cones and just touch the middle one ([CD 2026](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/how-to-run-driver-training-practice/518655)). For REBUILT, the drills worth isolating are trench transit with the hood down, parking on the three backup spots, and the come-home and climb timings in the checklist. A 2026 team targeted about 10 hours of full-court practice before its first event ([CD "Drive Practice"](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/drive-practice/514962)). 5669 will not get that, so the event itself becomes the practice block. Beach Blitz advertises "more matches per team" ([beachblitz.org](https://beachblitz.org/)).

## One game plan, same every match

REBUILT pays 1 point per FUEL, and only into an active hub. The hub is active for **110 of the 160 match seconds**: auto 20 s, a 10 s transition with both hubs open, four 25 s shifts that alternate, and a 30 s endgame with both open. The alliance that scores more FUEL in auto has its hub **closed in shift 1** and open in shifts 2 and 4. FUEL keeps counting for 3 s after a hub closes ([Game Manual TU22](https://firstfrc.blob.core.windows.net/frc2026/Manual/2026GameManual.pdf)). One coach summed up the planning: the auto winner gets about 35 s to collect before its first scoring shift, and the loser gets an extra 25 s to hoard for endgame ([CD "Rebuilt Coaching Advice"](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/rebuilt-coaching-advice/515940)). For a team that cannot improvise, the simplest strong plan is two phases, "fill while closed, empty while open," with several pre-planned spots so a defender can't sit on the one comfortable spot ([CD "Stop Overcomplicating REBUILT"](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/stop-overcomplicating-rebuilt/513157)). 5669's turret removes most of the alignment skill that thread worried about.

| Phase | Driver | Robot does by itself |
|---|---|---|
| **Auto (20 s)** | Hands off. The one tested auto; never fully cross the center line (G403). | Paths, aim, score |
| **Transition (10 s, both open)** | Stay inside our zone and score. | Aim already on; fires when ready |
| **Our shift (25 s)** | Score from inside the line (robot center within 4.19 m / 165 in of our wall). Use any spot, or the backup spots if the camera is in doubt: **Midshot** about 2.30 m (91 in) out from our hub toward our wall, or the **trench spots** about 1.05 m (41 in) out and 3.39 m (133 in) to either side. Sweep FUEL along our own wall and behind our hub between volleys. | Speed cap and X-lock while RT is held; holds shots that would land in a closed hub |
| **Their shift (25 s)** | One trip **through the trench** (not the bump) into the neutral zone. Collect, and let auto-aim pass home. Come back on "come home". | Picks PASS beyond the line (corner landing point 0.94 m / 37 in from our wall, 1.65 m / 65 in from the side wall), shuttles from their zone to a relay point 6.0 m (236 in) out, checks pass lanes against both hubs |
| **Endgame (30 s, both open)** | Full hopper, drive to the tested rung end on "climb now", climb, then **hands off the sticks**. | Climb shot table; vision held while hanging; protected by G420 |

The endgame choice runs against elite opinion, and the reason is worth knowing. At the top level climbing was called "a trap": "For 10 seconds, good teams can score 30 balls" ([CD endgame thread](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/so-about-this-rebuilt-endgame/515984)). But the same thread put the average early-season match at about **120 points**. That is roughly 40 per robot, or about 0.4 FUEL per active second, so a 10-point L1 is worth more than 20 s of an average robot's cycling. Climbs decided many close district matches, including one coach's report that "half of our quals wins at FSC North Charleston were determined by climbs" ([CD "Climb Wins 2026?"](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/climb-wins-2026/516377)). 5669 also gives up almost nothing by climbing. The tower sits in our alliance zone, 3.4 to 4.0 m (134 to 157 in) from the hub, so launching from it is legal under G407. The robot keeps shooting while hanging. And G420 makes touching a robot on its own tower in the last 30 s a MAJOR foul for the opponent ([Game Manual TU22](https://firstfrc.blob.core.windows.net/frc2026/Manual/2026GameManual.pdf)). Deep climbs took about 10 s in the season's reports ([CD L3 trap thread](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/so-how-do-we-not-spend-like-a-month-falling-for-the-l3-trap/519874)). Until the climb drill gives a real number, plan to start the climb by **0:20 to 0:15**. An auto climb (15 points) is lower priority, because it costs auto FUEL, and auto FUEL alone sets the shift order.

Most of the match-losing mistakes in REBUILT were fouls and traps, not missed shots. A week 1 referee's most-called fouls were contact inside an opponent's frame perimeter (G415), catching FUEL under the hub (G408), pins where the defender backed off less than **72 in (1.83 m)** so the count kept running (G418), and touching robots on their tower in endgame (G420). Passes from the neutral zone that accidentally scored drew G407 MAJORs ([CD common fouls](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/what-are-the-most-common-fouls-in-2026-rebuilt-so-far/515998)). Robots that flew over the bump onto a pile of FUEL got "beached" ([CD coaching advice](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/rebuilt-coaching-advice/515940)), and holding a robot on the bump is legal indefinitely under Q&A 207 ([CD bump trap](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/its-legal-to-trap-robots-on-the-bump/518294)). 5669's code already enforces G407 (the 4.19 m / 165 in line and the pass-lane check), holds fire on a closed hub, and warns for G418 and G420 by rumble. That leaves three habits for the driver, which go on the plan card: **intake up near an opponent, never under the hub while it is raining FUEL, trench instead of bump when full or defended.**

This plan also makes 5669 easy to pick. At SoCal events "basically every second pick was offensive in nature," with robots that counterfed during inactive shifts and scored during active ones ([CD 4-digit thread](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/is-a-4-digit-clean-score-possible-in-2026-2027-rebuilt/518723)). Strategists valued trench capability and "just flexibility" for second picks ([CD Outlier's take](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/an-outliers-take-on-the-26-worlds-strategic-situation/519681)). A turret robot that runs clean matches, passes visibly and climbs reliably shows scouts exactly that role.

## Three people, fixed words, every match debriefed

The split from the earlier control study still holds. **The coach tells the driver where to go and when; the operator tells the driver what the robot is doing.** With auto-aim now on by default, the operator's job is even less about the trigger and more about eyes and modes. The coach watches the macro picture (clock, score, partners, refs) and decides in advance who owns mid-match calls, and calls use words agreed beforehand, since people "don't assume everyone interprets terms the same way" ([FIRST Drive Coach Best Practices](https://community.firstinspires.org/hubfs/web/program/frc/resources/drive-coach-practices.pdf)). 5000's driver at Week 0 put it plainly: "I had a very hard time telling when the shift would change or even whose shift it was... having a drive coach communicate shift timings is very important" ([5000 #384](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/507502/384)). Good coaches call routes and cycles, such as "one more cycle, left trench back over bump and let it go," rather than steering the robot ([CD drive coach tips](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/drive-coach-tips/520822)). Add a fourth role, the **technician**, who owns the field connection, the systems check and "right auto selected" ([4946 wiki](https://wiki.4946.ca/Competitions/Drive-Team)). For 5669 that means Pit Check ALL GO, the Practice Field switch OFF, alliance set and Auto Winner on FMS.

| Plan card (one per match) | |
|---|---|
| Start spot, auto name | Read back by the technician |
| Who probably wins auto | Coach's guess, confirmed live from the hub lights |
| Our route out and back | e.g. "left trench out, left trench back" |
| Defense? | Who, and the call that ends it ("offense" = operator presses RB) |
| Climb | Rung end, "climb now" time from the drill |
| Partner deconfliction | Who takes which half of the neutral zone |

| Call | Answer |
|---|---|
| "Won auto, closed first" / "Lost auto, open first" | "Closed first" / "Open first" |
| "Ten to open" / "Ten to close", then "five, four, three, two, one" | Key word |
| "Come home" | "Home" |
| "Left trench," "Right trench," "Left bump," "Right bump" | Route name |
| "Cleanup alliance zone," "Behind our hub," "Our wall" | Place name |
| "Defend [number]" / "Offense" | "Defend" / operator: "Aim on" |
| "Climb now" / "Hands off" | "Climbing" / "Off" |
| "Full," "Half," "Empty" (operator) | Coach plans from it |
| "Midshot," "Left trench spot," "Right trench spot" (operator, camera trouble) | "Parking" |
| "Winner set" (operator, after the no-data buzz) | "Set" |

The listener repeats the key word, and no repeat means the call did not land. Defense keeps one rule from the earlier study, softened by the new build. The driver presses Y only to **start** defense, and the operator ends it with RB on the "offense" call. Defense ending by itself now gives the driver two short buzzes, and leaving defense turns auto-aim back on by itself.

Oct 30 is the only robot time 5669 will get at full size, so script it. Teams that miss practice matches can still get a field connection test ([FMS manual, Practice](https://fms-manual.readthedocs.io/en/latest/scorekeeper-reference/step-by-step/practice.html)). Beach Blitz runs load-in plus late-afternoon practice matches that day ([beachblitz.org](https://beachblitz.org/)). The order is connect and Pit Check, the chosen auto once, the two cycle routes and one pass, the missing shot points, then stop to protect the robot. After every match, cool down first, then debrief in two minutes with no blame and genuine praise outnumbering criticism, and fix any call that was misheard ([FIRST Drive Coach Best Practices](https://community.firstinspires.org/hubfs/web/program/frc/resources/drive-coach-practices.pdf)). A workable format is one thing to keep, one thing to change and one call to fix, plus the "Last Volley" scorecard and the shot log for the operator's shot notes. Between matches the coach can drill alone: pause a video of another team's REBUILT match and say the next call before playing it ([CD drive coach tips](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/drive-coach-tips/520822)).

## Conclusion

The useful reframe is that assist value runs opposite to practice. The best teams' "deprecated features" lists (snake mode, wall-intake snaps, trench centering) are a shopping list for a team in 5669's position, as long as each assist can be switched off and none of them surprises the driver. 5669 has now taken the cheap end of that list, and it also removed something worse than a missing assist: buttons that silently changed another person's mode. The remaining gains are not in code. They are in two measured times that turn the coach's guesses into calls, a script that makes every match look like the last, and an Oct 30 practice block spent on rehearsal, not experiments.

What stays unknown is how much any of this is worth in points. No team published assist-by-assist cycle data, the climb-versus-cycle break-even rests on early-season averages rather than 5669's own rate, and the deceleration conclusion uses an assumed wheel friction. Beach Blitz can answer all three. Log balls per active second, climb time and slip current at the event. The off-season decision on trench lock, single-driver controls and stopping then becomes a measured one.
