1Re-gear the spindexer to 16:1 robot OFF · 2 people · ~20 min

  1. Robot OFF (main breaker). Take a photo of how the spindexer motor + gearbox is mounted.
  2. Remove the motor + gearbox from the robot. The motor stays wired; let it hang carefully.
  3. Remove the long screws that hold the gearbox stages together (they come out of the output end).
  4. The gearbox is three 4:1 stages (4 × 4 × 4 = 64:1). Take out one 4:1 stage. Now it's 4 × 4 = 16:1 (about 4× faster).
  5. Stack it back. Twist by hand so the splines engage. No gap between the stack and the input plate.
  6. Use shorter screws that match 2 stages (a 3-stage stack uses 2.5 in screws; with one stage gone the old ones are too long and will leave it loose). Snug, not cranked.
  7. Re-mount it like the photo. Turn the omni wheel by hand: smooth, no clicking.
  8. Write 16:1 on the gearbox with a paint pen.
Why 16:1?
At 64:1 the 8 in omni wheel's rim moves about 1 m/s: balls crawl. At 16:1 it can do about 4 m/s, and we turn it down to the best speed in step 5 with a number on the dashboard. It is still far stronger than foam balls need (about 250 N of push at the rim).

2Power on the right way every time you power on

  1. Turret on its HOME mark.
  2. Hood pushed all the way down by hand.
  3. Intake all the way up. Elevator all the way down.
  4. Fresh battery (12.5 V or more). Main breaker ON. Connect the laptop (robot Wi-Fi or USB cable).
Why?
The turret, hood, intake and elevator call wherever they are at power-on "zero". Wrong spot at power-on = every position is wrong.

5Set the feed speeds enabled · ~15 min

Do this BEFORE the shot map: changing feed speed later changes every shot.

  1. Park about 3 m from the hub. Tab Shot Map: Shot Tuning Mode ON, then Operator RB (auto-aim ON).
  2. Tab Feed + Spots: type 8 into Spindex Forward Volts and press submit. Leave Uptake Forward Volts at 11.
  3. Load a full hopper. Press Reset Shot Stats. Hold Operator RT until it's empty.
  4. Read Shots At Speed. 90% or more: raise Spindex volts by 1 and repeat. Under 90% (or jams): lower by 1. Keep the fastest setting that stays at 90%+ with no jams.
  5. Watch the balls go up: if two go up touching, raise Uptake volts by 0.5. If they rattle going in, lower it by 0.5.
  6. Write down the final Spindex volts and Uptake volts for Marcos.
What "Shots At Speed" means
Each ball slows the flywheel for a moment. If the next ball comes before it speeds back up, that ball flies short. "9 of 10 (90%)" means 9 balls left at full speed. "Last Shot Recovery (s)" is how long the wheel needs between balls.

6Save the 3 backup spots ~5 min

  1. Drive to Midshot (right between our hub and the climb). Stop. Tab Feed + Spots: press Save Spot: Midshot. Backup Shot says SAVED.
  2. Drive to the left trench spot (left as seen from the driver station, robot poking out of the trench). Press Save Spot: Left Trench.
  3. Same for the right trench: Save Spot: Right Trench.
Says NOT SAVED?
The robot doesn't know where it is. Go back in front of the hub until Vision Status says OK SET, then drive to the spot and press again.

7Record the shot map enabled · 45 to 60 min

  1. Tab Shot Map. Read the big line at the top: Shot Map Step. Do exactly what it says. It walks you through every distance, closest first, and puts in good starting numbers.
  2. At each distance: hold RT for 4 balls. Short: raise Tune RPM by 100. Long: lower it by 100. Hitting the rim: change Tune Hood (rot) by 0.05. Change one number at a time.
  3. 3 of 4 go in? Press Record Shot Point. The checklist Shot Map Progress ticks that distance and the step moves on.
  4. Keep going until it says DONE! Then turn Shot Tuning Mode OFF.
Tips
Use the same battery level all session (swap below 12.3 V). Record in the middle of what works: if 4300 and 4500 both go in, record 4400. Messed one up? Drive back to that distance and record again: the new one replaces it.

8Test it for real ~10 min

  1. Shot Tuning Mode OFF, auto-aim ON (RB). From 3 different spots, hold RT: it should score from each.
  2. Backup shots: park on Midshot, press Operator A, hold RT. Then the left trench (Operator X) and the right trench (Operator LB).

For Marcos (and his Claude)
Everything recorded is already saved on the roboRIO, so the robot shoots right even before any code change. To make it permanent: paste the Shot Table Code line into ShotTable.defaults(), set Constants.Index.kForwardSpeed and Constants.ShooterIntake.kForwardVolts to the measured volts, build, deploy, push. The CSV columns are listed on its first line; CLAUDE.md explains the whole system.
